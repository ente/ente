# Share photos with family on iPhone and Android

Pick an app that works the same on both. iCloud Shared Albums used to be Apple-only. Apple says that starting in iOS 27, friends and family can join and add photos on the web without an Apple Account or device ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108314)). Google Photos works on iPhone, Android, and the web, and is the most common choice. Google's Gemini features in Photos may process the photos and videos you store there to improve edits or make inferences ([Google](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/15344015)). For private photos, choose an end-to-end encrypted app with iPhone and Android apps, such as Ente.

## What a family usually needs

- Everyone can add photos, not just view them.
- Grandparents can see photos without learning a new app.
- Full-quality photos, so you can print them later.
- One bill, not five.

## The main options

**Google Photos.** Works on iPhone, Android, and the web. Shared albums let everyone add photos, and partner sharing can share your whole library with one person ([Google help](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6131416)).

**iCloud Shared Albums.** Easy if everyone has an iPhone. Ente's [Import from Apple Photos](/photos/migration/from-apple-photos/) guide documents that shared-album copies are downscaled and lose location data. Apple says that on iOS 26 and earlier, downloaded shared albums may not contain the same information as the originals, and that starting in iOS 27 Shared Albums support full-resolution sharing ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108314)). Shared Albums aren't end-to-end encrypted, even with Advanced Data Protection on ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108756)).

**Ente.** iPhone and Android apps, plus web and desktop. Albums are end-to-end encrypted, including when you share them.

## Setting up Ente for a family

**1. Share one plan.** On a paid plan, add up to 5 family members to your storage at no extra cost, so 6 people share one subscription. Learn more in [Family plans](/photos/features/account/family-plans). Each person signs up for their own Ente account. Then add them under **Manage family** in your subscription settings. Everyone keeps a separate, private library by default.

**2. Share albums.** Create an album, for example "Family 2026", and share it with each person's Ente email. Give someone Viewer, Collaborator, or Admin rights. Collaborators can add their own photos, and everyone can comment and like. Learn more in the [Sharing and collaboration FAQ](/photos/faq/sharing-and-collaboration). Open `Settings > Family`, pick a family member, and tap **Share albums**.

**3. For relatives who won't install anything,** send a public link. It opens in a browser. You can add a password and an expiry date. The photos stay end-to-end encrypted. The key is in the link itself, so send it only to people you trust. Learn more in the [Sharing and collaboration FAQ](/photos/faq/sharing-and-collaboration#public-link-encryption).

## So which one?

- Your family already lives in Google's apps and privacy isn't a big worry: Google Photos.
- Everyone has an iPhone and you only need to show photos: iCloud Shared Albums.
- Mixed devices, private photos, and one shared plan: Ente.

## A few more answers

### Does everyone need to pay? {#family-sharing-who-pays}

No. Family plans come with paid subscriptions, and the person paying adds the others at no extra cost. Learn more in [Family plans](/photos/features/account/family-plans).

### Can family members see each other's whole library? {#family-sharing-separate-libraries}

No. Libraries stay separate unless you share albums with them.

### Do shared photos count against everyone's storage? {#family-sharing-storage}

Viewing a shared album doesn't. Saving shared photos into your own library does. In a collaborative album, each photo's storage counts toward the person who uploaded it. Learn more in the [Sharing and collaboration FAQ](/photos/faq/sharing-and-collaboration#shared-storage-count).
