# Share photos with family on iPhone and Android

When your family uses a mix of iPhones and Android phones, you need an app that works the same on both. Ente has apps for iPhone, Android, web and desktop, and keeps every album end-to-end encrypted, including shared ones.

## What a family usually needs

- Everyone can add photos, not just view them.
- Grandparents can see photos without learning a new app.
- Full-quality photos, so you can print them later.
- One bill, not five.

## Set up Ente for your family

**1. Share one plan.** On a paid plan, you can add up to 5 family members to your storage at no extra cost, so 6 people share one subscription. Each person signs up for their own Ente account, then you add them under **Manage family** in your subscription settings. Everyone keeps a separate, private library. Learn more in [Family plans](/photos/features/account/family-plans).

**2. Share albums.** Create an album, for example "Family 2026", and share it with each person's Ente email. To share with someone on your family plan, open `Settings > Family`, pick them, and tap **Share albums**. Give each person Viewer, Collaborator or Admin rights. Collaborators can add their own photos, and everyone can comment and like. Learn more in the [Sharing and collaboration FAQ](/photos/faq/sharing-and-collaboration).

**3. Send a link to relatives who won't install anything.** A public link opens in any browser, and you can add a password and an expiry date. The photos stay end-to-end encrypted, but the key is part of the link, so send it only to people you trust ([Sharing and collaboration FAQ](/photos/faq/sharing-and-collaboration#public-link-encryption)).

## Other options

**Google Photos.** Works on iPhone, Android and the web, and is the most common choice. Shared albums let everyone add photos, and partner sharing can share your whole library with one person ([Google](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6131416)). Google says its Gemini features in Photos may process the photos and videos you store there to improve edits or make inferences ([Google](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/15344015)).

**iCloud Shared Albums.** Easiest when everyone has an iPhone. Starting in iOS 27, friends and family can join and add photos on the web without an Apple Account, and Shared Albums support full-resolution sharing. On iOS 26 and earlier, downloaded shared albums may not contain the same information as the originals ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108314)). Ente's [Import from Apple Photos](/photos/migration/from-apple-photos/) guide describes those older copies as downscaled, with location data removed. Shared Albums aren't end-to-end encrypted, even with Advanced Data Protection on ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108756)).

## Which should you pick?

- If your family already uses Google's apps and privacy isn't a big concern, Google Photos is the easy choice.
- If everyone has an iPhone and you only need to show photos, iCloud Shared Albums will do.
- If you have mixed devices, want your photos private, and want one shared plan, choose Ente.

## FAQ

### Does everyone need to pay? {#family-sharing-who-pays}

No. Family plans come with paid subscriptions, and the person paying adds the others at no extra cost.

### Can family members see each other's whole library? {#family-sharing-separate-libraries}

No. Libraries stay separate unless you share albums.

### Do shared photos count against everyone's storage? {#family-sharing-storage}

Viewing a shared album doesn't. Saving shared photos into your own library does. In a collaborative album, each photo counts toward the storage of the person who uploaded it ([Sharing and collaboration FAQ](/photos/faq/sharing-and-collaboration#shared-storage-count)).
