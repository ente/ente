# Import from Proton Drive

Open Proton Drive on the web at [drive.proton.me](https://drive.proton.me), select the photos or folders you want, and click **Download**. When you download several items at once, Proton packs them into a ZIP ([Proton: How to set up Proton Drive](https://proton.me/support/set-up-proton-drive)). For a large library, download in batches and check the counts before deleting anything.

Proton Drive is end-to-end encrypted, so only you can open your files ([Proton](https://proton.me/support/set-up-proton-drive)).

## What you need

- A computer with enough free space for your whole library.
- Your Proton account password (and a two-factor code if you use one).
- Time for a large library. Plan for several sessions.

## Step 1: Find your photos

Check everywhere your photos might be: anything backed up from the Proton Drive phone app, and any folders you uploaded yourself.

## Step 2: Download from the web app

1. Go to [drive.proton.me](https://drive.proton.me) and sign in.
2. Open the folder you want.
3. Select the photos or folders. You can select several items at a time.
4. Click **Download**. Proton saves multiple items as a single ZIP.
5. Unzip into a folder on your computer, then download the next batch.

If a large ZIP fails, select fewer items. Proton's [Drive help](https://proton.me/support/drive) has the current details.

Proton has desktop apps for Windows and macOS ([Proton Drive download](https://proton.me/drive/download)). Proton's [Photos help](https://proton.me/support/drive/photos) doesn't spell out a full photo export from those apps, so use the web app.

## Step 3: Check dates and locations

Open a sample of photos and check that their dates and locations look right before you continue. Compare your local file count with what Proton shows.

## Step 4: Upload to Ente

Treat the downloaded folder like any other folder of photos. In the Ente desktop app, sign in and drag the folder in. To keep each subfolder as its own album, choose **Separate albums**.

Learn more in [Import from local hard disk](/photos/migration/from-local-hard-disk).

## Is moving from Proton Drive to Ente worth it?

That depends on what you want. Both are end-to-end encrypted, so you won't gain privacy by switching. People usually move because they want an app built for photos: face grouping, search, a map, and shared albums with family.

Face grouping, search, and the map run on your device, so Ente does not see your photos. Learn more in the [Security and privacy FAQ](/photos/faq/security-and-privacy#ml-privacy). Shared albums are end-to-end encrypted as well. Learn more in [Sharing](/photos/features/sharing-and-collaboration/share).

If you mostly store documents and only a few photos, staying with Proton is reasonable.

## When you're done

Keep your Proton copy until you've checked the new one. [Get the Ente desktop app](https://ente.com/download/) and drag your downloaded folders in.
